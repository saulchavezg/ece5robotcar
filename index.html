Control System

PID Tuning - Per Track

Values were adjusted via onboard potentiometers for each track. Proportional gain drove correction speed; derivative damped oscillation on curves.

Drag Race
P (Proportional)~50%
I (Integral)Off
D (Derivative)Off

Proportional-only control for maximum straight-line speed. No correction accumulation needed on a short, direct track.

Frequency Sweep
P (Proportional)Tuned
I (Integral)Tuned
D (Derivative)Tuned

Values varied across checkpoints; each segment of the track demanded different tuning, so settings were adjusted iteratively rather than fixed to a single configuration.

Loop
P (Proportional)~50%
I (Integral)Off
D (Derivative)~50%

Derivative term added to smooth the continuous circular path, dampening overcorrection and keeping the robot on track through the full loop.

Reflection

What We'd Do Differently

Lessons learned from hardware failures, scheduling constraints, and competition day surprises.

Our biggest hardware setback was wires coming unsoldered during testing, which cost us tuning time. In addition, soldering connections earlier in the process would have made our build far more reliable. Our motor driver also failed early on, which caused delays and forced us to re-verify the connections before we could continue tuning.

We also weren't able to complete our initial 3D print on time because the Envision Makerspace was full, and we only later realized we could have printed a light shield to improve sensor consistency across different lighting conditions. Our front wheel also developed a wobble during competition that we should have caught and swapped out sooner.

On the software side, track-specific code for the frequency sweep would have helped; other teams implemented reversing and braking at sharp direction changes that we didn't have. Despite this, we achieved victory, however.

That said, we're proud of the outcome: 1st on the frequency sweep, 2nd on the loop, and 3rd on the drag race. We believe we built the most versatile robot in the competition, despite our setbacks and our starting point. The only team that outpaced us significantly had optimized solely for drag, while we competed strongly across all three events.

While no one may read this, I, Saul Chavez, can state that I am proud of every member of my team. From what I know about each and every one of us, none of us come from affluence, nor do we come from environments where we could get a jumpstart on topics that would have made the building process easier. We all learned on the spot and showed up as a team. At all times, there was almost never a moment when a member worked on the robot alone; there were always at least two, and most of the time, all of us. So to whoever may read this, know that there is truth in willpowering your way through situations in which it may feel as if others are "smarter" or "better" in some ways, and know that teamwork does indeed make the dream work. We may not have had the most beautiful robot out there, and from the murmurs I heard about ours, I know there were doubts, but our passion and resilience ultimately overcame them.
